Three things every MMO player asks early on: what do I lose when I die, how long does it take to level, and do I *have* to find a group? Here's our plan.

## When you fall

There's no "You Died" screen and no button to press. The camera stays where you fell, a short countdown runs, and you come back at the **nearest Waystone** (or the region's city) with full health. You unlock Waystones just by walking near them.

| You lose | You keep |
|---|---|
| a small slice of XP towards your current level (you never lose a level) | **every item**: nothing drops in PvE |
| a bit of durability on your weapon and armour (repair at the Blacksmith) | your Bloodline, Mastery and runes |
| half of your Remnant gauge, and your food and potion buffs | |

**New players below level 15 lose nothing at all.** Learn the game without fear.

Don't want to walk back? During the countdown you can **revive on the spot** by spending a full Remnant gauge (open world only, with a long cooldown). It can't be bought.

In dungeons you respawn at the last checkpoint, and party members can pull you back up by standing next to you for a few seconds.

## Levelling to 60

The level cap is **60**, about **90 to 100 hours** of normal play: main quests plus some farming. The first playable version takes you from **level 1 to 15** in roughly three to four hours.

Most XP comes from fighting monsters, a big share from quests, and the rest from dungeons and world bosses. The main story can carry you on its own. Monsters far below your level give much less XP, so there's no point hiding in easy zones.

Your lifeskills and weapon Mastery level up separately and never count towards your character level.

## Parties: safer, never required

Parties hold up to five players. XP is shared with members nearby and within ten levels of each other, and it works out to **about the same XP per hour as playing solo**. A group isn't a shortcut; it's safer and more fun. A party with **different Bloodlines** gets a small XP bonus.

## Who gets the loot

| Where | Rule |
|---|---|
| Open world | the player who did the **most damage** owns the drops for a short time, then anyone can pick them up. In a party, ownership rotates between members |
| Dungeon bosses | **everyone gets their own roll**. Nothing to fight over |
| World bosses | everyone who helped gets a roll; the top damage dealers get extra |

*Numbers are our current plan and will be tuned in testing.*
