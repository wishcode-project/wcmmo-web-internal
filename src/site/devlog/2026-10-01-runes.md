Bloodlines change *how* you play. Runes are the fine-tuning on top: small passive bonuses that decide whether your Fury is a glass cannon or hard to kill.

## Slots

You start with **two rune slots**. A third opens at level 30 and a fourth at level 50. You can swap runes freely whenever you're out of combat, and the same rune can't be slotted twice.

## The four groups

| Group | Runes | What they're for |
|---|---|---|
| **Defence** | Vitality, Stoneskin, Steadfast, Warding | more health, less damage taken, a longer guard, elemental resistance |
| **Offence** | Edge, Arcana, Fletch, Precision, Ruin, Haste, Tempo, Slayer | physical, magic and arrow damage, critical hits, shorter cooldowns, faster basic attacks, extra damage to bosses |
| **Sustain** | Leech, Breath, Clarity | lifesteal, stamina and mana regeneration |
| **Utility** | Echo, Swiftness | a faster Remnant gauge, faster movement |

Every rune comes in **three tiers (I, II and III)**. Tier I drops from monsters in the low zones, tier II from harder zones and Alchemy, tier III from dungeons and high-level Alchemy.

## One Bloodline, many roles

Put Vitality, Stoneskin and Steadfast on a Ward and it becomes a wall. Put Edge, Precision and Ruin on the same Ward and it hits back hard. Leech and Breath let a Fury stay in the fight longer. That's the whole idea: the Bloodline stays the same, the role changes.

## Fair by design

Runes are **passives only**. New mechanics belong to Bloodlines, never to runes. And some bonuses share a limit with other systems, so no single stack can break the game: cooldown reduction is shared with Weapon Mastery, attack speed with your Agility, and critical chance with your gear.

## In the first playable version

The vertical slice has two slots and eight runes (tiers I and II): Vitality, Stoneskin, Edge, Arcana, Fletch, Haste, Leech and Breath. The rest follow.

*Rune values are starting points and will be tuned in testing.*
